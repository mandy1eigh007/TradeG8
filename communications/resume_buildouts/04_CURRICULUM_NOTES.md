# Curriculum Notes — Distilled from the Instructor Documents

Source: `contents/Resume -20260424T184228Z-3-001.zip` (extracted and read 2026-06-12).
These are the teaching documents Mandy uses in the ANEW classroom.

## Lesson plan (150 minutes, 5 phases)

Objective: "Students will create a professional, construction-focused resume
that demonstrates transferable skills, job-specific abilities, and readiness
for apprenticeship or employment opportunities."

1. Introduction & Discussion (20 min) — resume basics; construction-facing vs
   traditional differences.
2. Guided Practice (40 min) — students complete handout sections with support.
3. Drafting (30 min) — rough draft assembled from completed handouts.
4. Feedback & Revision (30 min) — instructor returns drafts, common issues.
5. Finalization (30 min) — final resume produced and printed.

Assessment: formative = handouts/participation/draft; summative = rubric-graded
final. Closure: each student shares one accomplishment they're proudest of.
**Pedagogical core: students never face a blank page — the resume is assembled
from pre-completed worksheet sections.**

## Student workshop modules (in order)

0. Framing: construction-facing vs traditional resume. "Show them what you can
   build, operate, or handle on a site. Prove you know safety and can work like
   an apprentice."
1. Why a resume matters — write what you want an employer to know in 10 seconds.
2. Header — name, phone, email, city/state. "An unprofessional email or old
   phone number can cost you an opportunity."
3. Objective — 2 scaffold questions → 1–2 sentence final.
4. Skills — 3 categories × at least 5 each (Transferable / Job-Specific /
   Self-Management).
5. Work experience — 3 jobs × at least 3 action-verb bullets.
6. Certifications — or "None yet" + plan.
7. Education — reverse chronological.
8. Optional sections — volunteer/other work if they make you stand out.
9. Build the draft — HEADER → OBJECTIVE → SKILLS → WORK EXPERIENCE →
   CERTIFICATIONS → EDUCATION.

Final checklist: one page · 10–12pt professional font · saved as PDF · peer
reviewed · instructor reviewed.

## Key content from the handouts

- **Combination resume format** is the recommended format for tradespeople
  (qualifications summary + detailed work history). Gaps are normal in
  construction; lead with qualifications and speak honestly to gaps.
- **20 named transferable skills** with construction examples (Adaptability,
  Attention to Detail, Leadership, Conflict Resolution, Work Ethic, Physical
  Stamina, Customer Service, Technical Learning, Organization, Initiative,
  Critical Thinking, Collaboration, Dependability, Regulatory Compliance,
  Manual Dexterity, Problem Anticipation, Resilience, Multi-Tasking, Learning
  from Feedback, Safety Leadership). Nontraditional backgrounds count: sports,
  military, caregiving.
- **Job-specific skills list**: power tools, blueprint reading,
  measuring/cutting, framing/siding/roofing basics, material handling,
  equipment operation, OSHA compliance, trades math. Advanced: laser
  levels/total stations, Procore/PlanGrid, rigging/crane signaling.
- **O*NET (onetonline.org)** recommended for rephrasing past job duties.
- **Online presence**: LinkedIn (trade-focused headline like "Aspiring
  Apprentice Carpenter"), Indeed profile + alerts, master resume tailored per
  posting with keywords.
- Real cautionary example: a student without an objective wasn't interviewed
  until she added one stating her target trade.

## Pace 59 — the 25-job translation library

25 prior occupations, each with: (1) "What You Did → What It Shows About You"
decoding table, (2) plain resume bullets, (3) construction-focused rewrite of
the same job. Occupations: office admin, retail/cashier, food service,
warehouse/delivery, janitorial, childcare/elder care, call center, security,
rideshare/delivery, bank teller, receptionist, housekeeper, personal trainer,
teacher/tutor, artist/maker, data entry, grocery, landscaper, pet care, event
staff, bartender, phone sales, personal assistant, stagehand/AV, volunteer.

Method: keep employer/dates/facts identical and truthful; re-describe duties in
site language emphasizing safety, physical capability, checklists, clean work
zones, independence, schedules, and team flow.

## Stand-Out Playbook essentials

- Philosophy: **"Evidence > adjectives."** Log quantities, measurements, prints
  followed, tools used, and who verified — turn that into bullets and stories.
- Credential truth-telling: OSHA 10/30 are Outreach cards, not certifications;
  forklift = employer evaluation on the specific truck (≤3 years); WA Flagger
  expires ≤3 years; EPA 608 "certification with teeth" — state the type.
- Trade-specific quick wins for Electrical (PSEJATC realities), HVAC/Pipe
  (UA 32, EPA 608), Outside Line/Tree (NW Line JATC ranking points, CDL, ISA).
- Contains the verbatim "What the App Must Do" integration list (recorded in
  `02_PRODUCT_DECISIONS.md`).

## The "App content report" caveat

`Professional Resume - App content report.docx` is NOT a feature spec — it's a
ChatGPT inventory of Mandy's personal document archive. Its useful design
pattern: a **verified career evidence vault** (resumes, recommendations, W-2
transcripts, school transcripts, licenses) from which a master evidence-backed
timeline and tailored resumes are generated. This matches the curriculum's
"master resume, tailor each version" rule and the hold-over "keep your paper
trail" rule — and maps to TradeG8's compliance/activity-log features.
