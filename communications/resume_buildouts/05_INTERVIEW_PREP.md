# Interview Prep — The Grilling Method

Source: "ChatGPT - Resume APP - Grilling Interview Questions" and
"ChatGPT-Resume_Refinement_Assistance" Drive exports (both are mock-interview
conversations; the second is misnamed). Read in full 2026-06-12.

## What it is

Covert, instructor-run panel mock interviews that audit a student's **research,
realism, and momentum** — "this is more of a grilling session. they just don't
know it." Purpose: verify the student actually knows what it takes to get into
their trade, not polish performance.

## The five-part strong-answer rubric (score every answer against this)

1. Names the exact trade or pathway.
2. Knows the current local/program requirements.
3. Explains the work accurately.
4. Shows current action, not fantasy.
5. Can prove momentum with documents, classes, cards, logs, or real applications.

Weak answers: "I just want to work hard." / "I like working with my hands." /
"I'm waiting to hear back." / "I'll figure it out when I get in." —
"That is not research. That is wishful thinking in steel-toe boots."
The standard: **"activity with receipts, not dreams with garnish."**

## The locked per-trade packet layout (one packet per trade, no page-flipping)

1. General apprentice fit
2. Pre-apprenticeship
3. Apprenticeship / local (exact documents, tests, intake steps)
4. Non-union / hold-over / L&I (trainee cards, countable hours)
5. CITC / open-shop (where it exists)
6. City / utility (Seattle City Light, Tacoma Power, SPU…)
7. Ranking / "what are you doing while you wait"
8. Instructor cheat sheet — answer key + rank-building activities at the bottom,
   so co-instructors who don't know the trade can run mocks.

Lineman and tree trimmer nest under the electrician umbrella. Per-trade .docx
packets were generated for Boilermakers, Bricklayers/BAC, Carpenters, Cement
Masons — remaining trades follow the same locked template.

## The three universal push-harder follow-ups (under every question)

1. "Be specific. What exactly did you do in the last 30 to 60 days?"
2. "What proof do you have right now?"
3. "What exact local, training center, contractor, city program, or employer
   are you talking about?"

## Signature question types (examples verbatim)

- Reality check: "What does this trade actually do day to day, not the romantic
  version — the real version?"
- Fit: "Why this trade specifically, and why not the two closest trades next
  to it?"
- Process: "Walk me through the application process in order. No guessing."
- L&I literacy: "If a contractor offers you work tomorrow, how will you check
  whether they are active with L&I and not just talking slick?" / "What records
  would you keep yourself so your hours do not disappear later?"
- Momentum: "If I asked you to show me proof of momentum today, what would you
  put on the table?"
- Traps: "If you are open to anything, name the three trades you researched
  most and give me one reason you eliminated the others."

## Standard closing battery (ask before ending any mock)

1. What exact trade/path are you targeting?
2. What exact local/program/employer/city path are you talking about?
3. What are the current minimum requirements?
4. What does the work actually look like?
5. What are you doing while you wait?
6. What proof do you have?
7. If you took non-union work tomorrow, how would you make sure it helps you
   legally and strategically?
8. If your plan falls through, what is your second and third pathway?

## Proof checklist (instructors literally ask students to produce documents)

Application confirmation · transcript or GED · math placement · CDL
permit/license · First Aid/CPR · flagger card · driving abstract · EPA 608
certificate or test registration · BEAM completion/registration · job
logs/pay stubs/reference letters · info-session attendance · ranking or
interview emails · saved posting screenshots.

## Red flags (instructor cheat sheet)

Cannot name the exact local/program · doesn't know required documents/tests ·
"waiting" with no proof of movement · confuses hold-over work with countable
hours · hasn't checked contractor legality or training-agent status · only
knows wage numbers, nothing about the work.

## Mode 2 — scripted answer coaching (for real interviews)

For a specific student facing a real interview: word-for-word answers per
expected question, each anchored in true experience with one quantified proof
point; a one-line fallback pitch if they blank; an explicit do-not-say list
("I want out of the field," "this place is toxic" — even if true); memory
joggers tagged "use the ones that are true for you"; then mock role-play.
"Give her the words so she doesn't have to think under pressure."
